Two rhythms: Wave and Loop

Both clinics follow the same screen, triage and treat pathway. They differ in how many women they serve and how quickly treatment follows.

Wave: high volume, two days

For sites that many women can reach, like towns and cities.

How a Wave clinic works

  1. Day 1Self-sample and HPV testWomen take their own sample in private. All samples are tested together in one batch.
  2. Day 2Stain and remote reviewHPV-positive women return for a cervical sample, stained by our automated Pap stainer and read through the portal.
  3. Day 2Treatment as results arriveCIN2 and CIN3 are treated with thermal ablation. Suspected cancers are referred.

For smaller or remote communities, where women are treated in one visit.

How a Loop clinic works

  1. MorningSelf-sample and rapid HPV testEach woman takes her own sample and gets her HPV result in about an hour.
  2. AfternoonStain and remote reviewHPV-positive women give a cervical sample, stained by our automated Pap stainer and read through the portal.
  3. AfternoonTreatment before she goes homeCIN2 and CIN3 are treated with thermal ablation. Suspected cancers are referred.

Autostainer

GyroStainer

A compact auto-stainer that applies the Pap stain consistently, so slides are easier to read and results more reliable. Other staining protocols, such as H&E or Giemsa, can also be programmed and run on GyroStainer.

Inside every Wave and Loop clinic, or available separately. Existing laboratories and clinics can use GyroStainer to standardise their staining, enabling faster and high-accuracy results.

Ask about GyroStainer

Our pre-validated Pap stain protocol

GyroStainer carries up to four slides through every stain, rinse and dehydration bath with precise timing, then dries them ready for scanning. Follow the full protocol below, sped up.

    Telecytology portal

    Our trusted network of pathologists reviews every slide remotely and returns results quickly, so women can be treated straight away. No pathologist is needed on site.

    Inside every Wave and Loop clinic, or available separately. Clinics and laboratories without a pathologist can send their slides through the portal and receive expert results quickly.

    From stained slide to result

    1. DigitiseA digital microscope scans the stained slide into a high-resolution image.
    2. UploadThe image is uploaded to the portal automatically, with the clinical information needed for a diagnosis.
    3. Review and returnA pathologist from our network reads the slide. The result and recommendation go straight back to the site, ready for treatment.

    A simple interface

    What a pathologist sees: pick a case, look closer at the slide, choose a result and send the report.

    In development

    Using machine learning to speed up review

    With EPFL's LTS5 lab, we are developing an algorithm that analyses every digitised slide and points the pathologist straight to the most suspicious cells. The pathologist always makes the diagnosis.

    Research preview
    1. 1DivideThe digital slide is split into small tiles.
    2. 2AnalyseEvery cell in every tile is isolated and given an abnormality score.
    3. 3RankThe cells with the highest scores are flagged, up to 40 per slide.
    4. 4ReviewThe pathologist goes straight to the flagged cells and makes the diagnosis.
    About 1 hourto review a full slide image by hand
    2 minutesfor the algorithm to analyse a slide
    About 10 minutesto reach a diagnosis by going straight to the flagged cells

    Early research figures. Once validated, the algorithm will be built into the portal at no extra cost to our partners.
