From HPV test to treatment, close to home.

CareVix brings cervical cancer screening, on-site triage and treatment of precancer to communities far from a laboratory, in a mobile unit or a fixed clinic. We work alongside NGOs and funders to make it happen.

Pink, teal and violet: the colours of a Pap-stained slide, and of CareVix.

In 2022, cervical cancer took the lives of 348,189 women.

Almost 94% of those deaths were in low- and middle-income countries, though the disease is one of the most preventable cancers.

Results take weeks

Samples travel to a central laboratory. Many women cannot make the long journey back for their results and are lost before treatment.

Too few specialists

Reading cytology needs trained cytotechnologists and pathologists, who are scarce and concentrated in city hospitals.

Manual staining varies

Pap staining by hand depends on timing and handling. Inconsistent slides are harder to read and less reliable.

Sources: Bray F. et al., Global cancer statistics 2022 (GLOBOCAN), CA Cancer J Clin, 2024. World Health Organization, Global strategy to accelerate the elimination of cervical cancer, 2020.

One visit, one pathway

Follow a sample from collection to care. Every step happens on site, except the expert review, which happens remotely.

  1. 1

    Sample

    A nurse explains the process and collects a cervical sample in a private consultation.

  2. 2

    HPV test

    The sample is tested for high-risk HPV, the screening method the WHO recommends. Most women test negative and go home reassured.

  3. 3

    Staining on site

    A positive HPV result alone doesn't mean disease. For these women, a slide is prepared and stained automatically by our GyroStainer, right where the sample was taken.

  4. 4

    Remote expert review

    The slide is digitised and read by cytology experts through a telecytology platform, so no specialist needs to be on site.

  5. 5

    Treatment on site

    Women with precancerous lesions (CIN2 and CIN3) are treated by the same team, without a second trip to a city hospital.

  6. 6

    Referral when needed

    Suspected cancers are referred to a hospital for specialised care.

GyroStainer: lab-quality slides, outside the lab

Our compact robot stains up to six Pap slides per run in about twenty minutes, using standard beakers and open reagents. It was tested on patient slides at the Geneva University Hospitals.

GyroStainer is part of every CareVix setup and is also available on its own for cytology laboratories.
