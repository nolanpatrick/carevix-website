Lab-quality cytology, close to the point of care.

UnaVix brings automated slide staining and remote review by pathologists to clinics without a laboratory or an on-site pathologist. With cytology as the triage step, cervical screening programmes can screen, triage and treat women in a single visit.

Partner with us Our solutions

In 2022, cervical cancer took the lives of 348,189 women1.

Almost 94% of those deaths were in low- and middle-income countries1. It is one of the most preventable cancers2, but many women there cannot reach the services that would prevent it.

Three gaps stand between these women and care:

Results take weeks

Samples travel to a central laboratory. Many women cannot make the long journey back for their results and are lost before treatment.

Too few pathologists

Reading cytology needs trained cytotechnologists and pathologists, who are scarce and concentrated in city hospitals.

Manual staining varies

Pap staining by hand depends on timing and handling. Inconsistent slides are harder to read and less reliable.

Sources

  1. Bray F. et al., Global cancer statistics 2022: GLOBOCAN estimates of incidence and mortality worldwide for 36 cancers in 185 countries, CA Cancer J Clin, 2024.
  2. World Health Organization, Global strategy to accelerate the elimination of cervical cancer as a public health problem, 2020.

The full pathway in a single visit

We support the WHO's screen, triage and treat approach. Our tools bring high-quality cytology triage on site, so clinics and screening programmes can take a woman from her first test to treatment in one visit.

1 · Screen

HPV self-test

Each woman takes her own sample in private, and the clinic tests it for high-risk HPV.

2 · Triage Our tools

Cytology, read remotely

A cervical sample from HPV-positive women is stained on site by GyroStainer, our automated stainer, and read by a pathologist through our telecytology portal.

3 · Treat

Thermal ablation

Women with precancerous lesions are treated by the clinic team in the same visit. Suspected cancers are referred to a hospital.

Next step

Pilot in Cameroon

As part of a study led by the Geneva University Hospitals (HUG), we will test GyroStainer and the telecytology portal in the field.

Objective

What we measure

  • The staining quality of GyroStainer
  • How well the telecytology portal works, and how easy it is to use

Design

How we test it

100 manually stained slides compared with 100 GyroStainer-stained slides, in the field in Cameroon.

Expected outcome

What we expect

  • Staining as good as manual staining, or more consistent
  • Faster results, with the portal automating slide transfers
