Our focus: cervical cancer screening

Cervical cancer screening is the first use of our tools. A single-visit model reduces loss to follow-up, but it usually means giving up high-quality triage. With our tools, clinics and screening programmes can keep cytology as the triage step and still screen, triage and treat women in a single visit.

The problem The pathway Step by step Why on site Why cytology Thermal ablation

In 2022, cervical cancer took the lives of 348,189 women1.

Almost 94% of those deaths were in low- and middle-income countries1. It is one of the most preventable cancers2, but many women there cannot reach the services that would prevent it.

Three gaps stand between these women and care:

Results take weeks

Samples travel to a central laboratory. Many women cannot make the long journey back for their results and are lost before treatment.

Too few pathologists

Reading cytology needs trained cytotechnologists and pathologists, who are scarce and concentrated in city hospitals.

Manual staining varies

Pap staining by hand depends on timing and handling. Inconsistent slides are harder to read and less reliable.

The full pathway in a single visit

We support the WHO's screen, triage and treat approach. Our tools bring high-quality cytology triage on site, so clinics and screening programmes can take a woman from her first test to treatment in one visit.

1 · Screen

HPV self-test

Each woman takes her own sample in private, and the clinic tests it for high-risk HPV.

2 · Triage Our tools

Cytology, read remotely

A cervical sample from HPV-positive women is stained on site by GyroStainer, our automated stainer, and read by a pathologist through our telepathology portal.

3 · Treat

Thermal ablation

Women with precancerous lesions are treated by the clinic team in the same visit. Suspected cancers are referred to a hospital.

Follow a sample step by step

Follow a sample, step by step

Every step can happen on site, in a single visit, using methods recommended by the WHO.

  1. 1 · Screen

    Self-sample

    A nurse explains how to take the sample. Each woman then collects a vaginal swab herself, in private, with no speculum exam.

    Many women are more willing to be screened when they can take the sample themselves. The WHO recommends self-sampling for HPV screening, and it lets a small team screen many women in a day.

  2. 2 · Screen

    HPV test

    High-risk HPV causes about 99% of cervical cancers3, and the WHO recommends HPV testing as the primary screening method. The sample is tested on site.

    Rapid HPV tests give a result in about an hour, so triage and treatment can follow the same day. About 8 in 10 women test negative4.

  3. 3 · Triage Our tools

    On-site Pap stain

    Most HPV infections clear on their own, so a positive test shows a woman is at risk, not that she has a lesion. Triage finds the women who actually need treatment.

    A nurse collects a cervical sample and prepares a slide. GyroStainer, our automated stainer, then applies a Pap stain consistently on site.

  4. 4 · Triage Our tools

    Remote review

    The stained slide is scanned and uploaded automatically to our telepathology portal, where a remote pathologist from our network analyses it.

    The portal is designed to return the result in under an hour, so the woman can receive immediate treatment.

  5. 5 · Treat

    Treatment on site

    Women with precancerous lesions (CIN2 and CIN3) are treated straight away with thermal ablation, in a few minutes and usually without anaesthesia. More about thermal ablation

  6. 6 · Refer

    Referral when needed

    Suspected cancers, and lesions that can't be treated with thermal ablation, are referred to a hospital for specialised care.

Screen

Why invest in on-site pathology if the world is moving to HPV testing?

Following the WHO's recommendation5, screening programmes are moving to HPV testing as the primary screen. Women who test positive still need triage, and doing it on site means they get their result in the same visit.

Fewer slides need a smaller machine

With HPV testing first, only the women who test positive need a slide: about 2 in 10 in a screening study in Cameroon4. Laboratory stainers are built for hundreds of slides a day. GyroStainer stains six slides per run at low cost, which suits the few triage slides a clinic prepares, and the telepathology portal brings the pathologist to them.

More than cervical screening

The same stainer runs H&E for histology and Giemsa for fine-needle aspiration cytology, so it keeps serving the clinic for other pathology exams, not only cervical screening.

Triage

Why cytology, and not VIA or histology

An HPV test is very sensitive, so the triage step that follows has to be specific: it should pick out the women who really need treatment. Cytology looks at the cells themselves, like histology, but does not require a biopsy.

The trouble with VIA

Visual inspection with acetic acid is cheap and immediate, but it depends on the eye and experience of the examiner. Reported specificity ranges from 65% to 98%6, so in some settings many women are treated who don't need it.

What holds cytology back, and how we fix it

Cytology is consistently specific (86–100%6), but its accuracy depends on slide quality and on the reader, and it usually needs a city laboratory, with results in days or weeks. Our automated stainer makes the staining consistent on site, and the telepathology portal brings an expert reader to every slide.

Treat

Thermal ablation

What it is

A portable device heats a metal probe to about 100 °C. The probe is applied to the abnormal area of the cervix for 20 to 40 seconds at a time, which destroys the precancerous cells7. The tissue then heals on its own over the following weeks.

Between women, the probe is cleaned and disinfected by boiling.

Why we use it

The WHO recommends thermal ablation to treat cervical precancer. The device is small and battery-powered, the procedure takes minutes and usually needs no anaesthesia, and trained nurses or doctors can perform it. That means women can be treated on site, in the same programme that screened them.

It suits lesions that are fully visible and not too large.
